Mubazi: AI-assisted water meter reading and billing

Technical report, final product. Dushime Paulette, BSc Software Engineering (Machine Learning), African Leadership University. Supervisor: Samiratu Ntoshi.
Live app: wasac-smart-billing-qt9t.vercel.app · Code: github.com/Dushimepaulette1/wasac-smart-billing

1. Overview

WASAC water meters in Kigali are read by hand, which is slow and leads to estimated bills, misread digits and late discovery of leaks. Mubazi lets a household photograph its meter. Computer vision reads the eight wheels, the household confirms them, the reading is checked against that household's history, and a suspicious reading goes to a WASAC officer before any bill is made. The main objective in the proposal was a prototype that improves the accuracy, traceability and timeliness of readings. Because the reader is right on only 55.7% of unseen photos, the central design decision is that AI assists and people decide.

3. Technical functionality

Figure 1. System architecture.

Architecture. A React and TypeScript single-page app (Vercel) calls a FastAPI service (Render, Docker) that serves three ONNX models with no PyTorch in the image, and stores data in PostgreSQL (Neon). Files are separated into routes, services, models, schemas, and anomaly and vision packages.

  • Reading. POST /submit-photo runs YOLO, the quality gate and the reader. The household confirms the wheels on an editable counter.
  • Check first. POST /check-reading runs the same read-only evaluate_reading as /confirm-reading, so the household learns whether a reading would be billed, held, or refused before it is stored. /confirm-reading repeats every check with the meter row locked.
  • Review and billing. A reading below the last one, three or more times usual use, a first reading with no start, a value typed well below a reliable AI read, a rollover, or an odd location is held. An accepted reading creates a bill with the tariff version recorded.
  • Payment. With MTN keys, MTN's status settles a payment, and an uncertain answer leaves it open rather than risking a second prompt. Otherwise the household dials WASAC's MoMo Pay code and gives the transaction ID, by app or USSD, and an officer confirms it against WASAC's merchant account.
  • Corrections. Billed readings are never overwritten. Adjustments recalculate the bill and the next one; anything that raises a charge needs a second person.

The forest's F1 was 0.394 higher than the rules' (paired 95% CI 0.251 to 0.566). The rules caught none of the 36 bypass months, because the stuck-meter rule needs a true zero, while the forest caught 32. The rules also flagged 28.8% of normal commercial months, because the 5 m³ a day limit was set for households. These figures show that the method can work and where the rules are weak, not how it would perform in Kigali: the faults were designed by the author, and the forest's 5% contamination setting equals the injected rate, which real data would not reveal. The feature consecutive_unpaid_months was dropped, because one unpaid bill disconnects a simulated customer at once, so it is always 0 in the active months scored.

Different inputs. Tests cover a normal reading, a lower reading, three and ten times usual use, a first reading, meter rollover, repeats within 25 days, the same photo sent twice, wrong sign-in codes, malformed or reused transaction IDs, bad CSV rows, and MTN timeouts, 5xx and 404 answers. Each has an expected response; the table is in docs/testing/README.md.

What testing changed. Review found that an abandoned MoMo Pay attempt blocked bill corrections and held back credit, and that a top-up after a correction looked like a double payment. Each was reproduced with a failing test, then fixed. Testing also found that a meter added with no first reading billed the whole register, that readings at 3 to 10 times usual use were billed, and that blur checks fired more on readable photos, so they were turned off.

5. Code quality practices

  • Abstraction and polymorphism. PaymentProvider is an abstract class with API, MoMo Pay and simulated versions, so start_payment never branches on a name. PyTorch and ONNX model classes can be swapped behind the same pipeline calls.
  • Encapsulation. BillBalance is the single definition of what is owed; dataclasses such as AnomalyResult and Evaluation hold results; each service has its own error class.
  • Separation of concerns and SOLID. Routes only translate HTTP; rules live in services. anomaly is split into scoring (pure), evaluation (read-only) and service (writes). Adding a provider needs a new class, not edits to the payment flow.
  • Reuse and maintainability. Typed API client, shared UI components, CSS design tokens, one read-only check for preview and submit. CI runs a strict type check. Naming, docstrings and folder layout are consistent.
  • Algorithms. CTC decoding with digit confidence, per-household features, a reset-aware history, tier walking, oldest-first credit settlement, and row locks with idempotency keys. See docs/ALGORITHMS.md.

6. Limitations and next steps

Accuracy is moderate on a small dataset, and the interval is wide. Thresholds and tariffs are provisional. The anomaly model has no real data, and the billing rules in its simulation, including a 20,000 RWF penalty and disconnection after one unpaid bill, are assumptions; SMS and USSD need live accounts, and free hosting is slow after sleep. The Kinyarwanda text awaits review by a native speaker. Next steps are read-only access to WASAC's customer and meter records under a data-sharing agreement that complies with Law No. 058/2021, a real pilot, and retraining on those data.
